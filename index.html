    How it works

    Both Mississippi and Tennessee give school districts extra money for students in poverty. Tennessee's funding formula (TISA) adds 25 percent of the base amount for each economically disadvantaged student. Mississippi's formula (MSFF) adds 30 percent for each low-income student, plus 10 percent for low-income students above 35 percent of a district's enrollment.

    Both states decide who counts mainly through direct certification: students whose families receive SNAP or TANF, plus students who are homeless, in foster care or migrant. This dashboard tests a broader measure. It combines census and USDA data on each district into an index of need, then shows how the same poverty money would move if that index decided which districts have the most need.

    What stays fixed. Everything set in law: the base amount, the weights, and the state's total number of students who count. You choose only which kinds of need matter and how much.

    Choose indicators, weight them, pick a scaling method, and see where the money goes. The map and rankings update as you go.

    Each state is ranked on its own. Use the switch above to change states. Downloads are at the bottom of the page, and sources and methods are in the README.

        How the students are re-shared
        1. Districts are ordered by your index, highest need first.
        2. The state's actual district rates are sorted from highest to lowest and handed out in that order. The district your index ranks first gets the highest real rate, and so on.
        3. Each rate is multiplied by the district's K-12 enrollment. The counts are then scaled so the statewide total matches the actual total, and no district can have more students than it enrolls.
        4. The law's weights are applied to the new counts.
